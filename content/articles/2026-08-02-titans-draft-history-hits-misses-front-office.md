## The Pattern Nobody Wants to Say Out Loud

The Titans have drafted well. Mostly.

Keith Bulluck. Albert Haynesworth. Michael Roos. Jurrell Casey. Kevin Byard. These aren't flukes. These are football players built by a franchise that understood line of scrimmage before it was fashionable to say so.

But there's a hole in the middle of all that competence. And it wears a helmet with a number under 20.

## They've Always Found the Trench Guys

Go back through the hits and a trend emerges. Tennessee identifies value in the second and third rounds better than most organizations. Byard in the fifth round in 2016 is the clearest example. A safety who became the defensive backbone of a franchise that didn't always deserve him.

Roos at left tackle. Casey inside. Bulluck as a linebacker who played with his hair on fire for a decade.

This front office — through multiple regimes — has trusted the scout who says a guy from a mid-major will outwork everyone. That instinct has been right more than wrong.

## The Quarterback Problem Is a Willpower Problem

Here's the part nobody puts in a press release.

The misses at quarterback aren't bad luck. They're decisions. Jake Locker in the first round in 2011 — athletic, injury-prone, and gone in four years. Marcus Mariota, the number two overall pick in 2015, showed flashes and then showed the door. Malik Willis in 2022. Third round. Turns out he needed more development than a team in win-now mode could give him.

Every single time, the Titans have drafted quarterbacks who needed something the organization wasn't equipped to provide. Patience. A true quarterback whisperer on the coaching staff. A plan beyond *let's see what happens*.

That's not scouting failure. That's organizational failure.

## What the Misses Have in Common

The draft busts that hurt — Locker, Corey Davis, the wide receivers who never became anything — share one trait. They were picks made under pressure. Picks made to answer a question the front office should have answered before draft night.

When Tennessee scouts quietly, when they trust their own board, they find football players.

When they draft to fill a hole in the fanbase's patience? They find trouble.

## The Next Question

Will Caleb Williams' class haunt them? No. They didn't pick there.

But Will Levis is the new chapter in this same old story. A big arm. A shaky supporting cast. A front office that picked him and then seemed surprised by what they got.

The Titans have always been better at building a team than building a quarterback. Until they solve that, the draft history reads like a good novel with a bad ending.
