## The Argument Is Simple

Tennessee is playing its first-time starting quarterback in 2026. It might be George MacIntyre. It might be Faizon Brandon. Nobody knows yet, including Josh Heupel.

What Tennessee does know is who is running the football. DeSean Bishop is back for his redshirt junior season, and he is the single player this offense cannot replace. Whoever is under center will have an easier time of it because of him.

## What Bishop Did Last Season

Eleven hundred and seventy-six yards. That's Bishop's 2025 rushing total — fourth in the SEC, first-team All-SEC, and the number that matters most: he was Tennessee's first running back to crack 1,000 rushing yards since Jalen Hurd in 2016.

Think about that decade-long gap. Tennessee has had good running backs in that stretch. They haven't had one who stayed healthy, stayed on the field, and handled the volume that Bishop handled.

He earned preseason All-SEC recognition for 2026 on the back of that performance. Media voters don't hand those out to running backs who are going to decline.

## What He Means for the Quarterback Battle

Both MacIntyre and Brandon are walking into something genuinely difficult: a nine-game SEC schedule with first-start nerves, a new defensive coordinator to go against in practice, and the weight of replacing the most scrutinized player in program history.

Bishop removes some of that difficulty.

An offense with Bishop can be built around protection. First-down runs that move the chains. Short passes that have room to run after the catch. Screens that use his vision and burst. The quarterback doesn't have to be Danny Wuerffel every Saturday — he has to be competent and protect the football.

That's a different job description than an offense built around spread concepts with a gunslinger who can make mistakes and still generate explosive plays. It is a more forgiving job description, and it is the job that Heupel is building for whoever wins the starting role.

## The Offensive Line Makes It Work

Bishop doesn't operate in isolation. He's running behind Wendell Moe Jr., who allowed one sack in 689 offensive snaps in 2025. He's running behind David Sanders Jr., who as a true freshman started nine games and didn't allow a sack in eight of them.

That's not a line that's figuring itself out. That's a line that knows what it's doing.

The question for Bishop in 2026 isn't whether he can produce. It's whether the offense around him can keep defenses from loading the box. With a proven quarterback, the answer was yes. With an unproven one, opposing defensive coordinators will be tempted to dare the passer.

That's the one variable Bishop can't control from his position. But he can make it very expensive for defenses to try.

## The Schedule Sets Up Around Him

Look at September. Tennessee plays Furman, Georgia Tech, and Kennesaw State before the SEC schedule opens with Texas at home on September 26. Three games to get the first-year starter comfortable, build rhythm with Bishop, and let the offensive line establish a running identity.

By the time Alabama comes to Neyland on October 17, Bishop should have enough game touches to be operating at his best. That matters because Alabama's defensive front will be the most serious physical test of his season.

## What the Film Shows

Bishop's 2025 tape demonstrates two things that don't show up in the yardage totals.

First, he makes the first tackler miss at a rate that exceeds his draft projections. He is not a power back who falls forward — he is a back who extends plays by avoiding contact and then finishing with it. That combination is rarer than it sounds.

Second, his ability to read the blocking scheme is advanced for his experience level. He is not waiting for the hole to open and then running to it. He is anticipating the block's direction and positioning himself to go through the gap before it exists.

Those traits get better with reps. He is going to be better in 2026 than he was in 2025.

## The Bottom Line

When the season ends and Tennessee evaluates whatever it is — a CFP bid, a bowl game, a near-miss — Bishop will be the player whose performance tracks most directly with the team's overall outcome.

Not the quarterback. Not Arion Carter. Not Jim Knowles.

Bishop. Because if he runs like he ran in 2025, this team is capable of winning any game on the schedule. And if he doesn't, the burden on a first-year starter becomes something no coaching staff would want to put on a young quarterback.

He is the most important player on this roster. The debate is about who throws to him. There is no debate about him.
