## The Building Nobody Bragged About

Nissan Stadium opened in 1999. The Titans were fresh off a move from Memphis. The city was still pinching itself. The stadium felt like a promise.

Twenty-five years later, that promise had turned into a leaking roof, flooded bathrooms, and locker rooms a Division II program would be embarrassed to use.

You don't have to take my word for it. Players said it. Coaches said it. Visiting scouts said it with their eyes when they walked through the tunnels.

## What The Building Actually Was

Here's what people miss about this conversation. Nissan Stadium wasn't just old. It was *deliberately* old. The Titans and the city let it age in place because neither side wanted to blink first on who would pay to fix it.

That's not neglect born of ignorance. That's a negotiating tactic stretched across a decade. The building was the leverage.

Meanwhile, real people — season ticket holders, working families who saved up for one game a year — sat in broken seats and used bathrooms that smelled like 1987.

The building suffered. The fans suffered. The franchise's recruiting pitch suffered. Nobody in the front office sat in those seats.

## What The Delay Cost

Talent notices. A quarterback choosing between franchises isn't just looking at cap space and coaching staff. He's looking at the facility. He's looking at whether ownership treats the operation like a real business or a tax write-off.

Nissan Stadium, in its final years, sent a message. The message wasn't flattering.

The Titans have drafted well and coached inconsistently since the Steve McNair era. But organizational culture starts at the top and works its way down through brick and mortar. A crumbling stadium is a crumbling statement of intent.

## The New Stadium Isn't A Gift

The new stadium — now rising on the East Bank — will be world-class. The renderings look like something out of a city that believes in itself.

Good. Nashville earned it. The fans earned it.

But let's not rewrite history. The replacement wasn't generosity. It was overdue maintenance dressed up in $2.1 billion worth of glass and steel. Public money went into it. Taxpayers deserve to hold that fact.

## The Small Truth

Every franchise eventually builds a monument to what it thinks it is. Nissan Stadium was honest, in its own ugly way — it showed exactly how much the Titans valued the experience of the people paying to watch.

The new building is another chance to say something different.

We'll see if they mean it.
