## The Number They Hung On Him

Shuler finished fourth in Heisman voting in 1993. Tennessee fans remember that season like a photograph — the option game, the arm, the way he moved in the pocket like he had somewhere to be. He was 9-1-1 as a starter his final year. The Vols lost to Florida and tied LSU and still he looked like the best quarterback in the country on certain Saturdays.

Washington took him third overall. Behind Dan Wilkinson. Ahead of Marshall Faulk.

That context matters.

## What Actually Happened

Shuler threw 33 touchdowns and 37 interceptions across three NFL seasons. Washington went 3-13 his first year. He lost his starting job to Gus Frerotte. Gus Frerotte, who later headbutted a padded wall celebrating a touchdown and gave himself a concussion.

That is not a joke. That actually happened.

Shuler got traded to New Orleans, where he backed up Jim Everett and then Billy Joe Hobert. He retired at 26 with a foot injury and a career quarterback rating of 56.7.

## The Misread That Matters

Here is the thing people get wrong about Shuler. They say he was a bust because he couldn't read defenses. That is true but incomplete.

Shuler ran a run-first, option-heavy offense at Tennessee under Phillip Fulmer. His completion percentage his senior year was 54 percent. The Vols threw the ball 20 times a game, sometimes less. He was accurate enough for what they asked, but nobody asked him to dissect a Cover 2 in the pocket under pressure with two seconds.

Washington paid him like a franchise quarterback and handed him a broken roster. The Redskins were 3-13 the year before they drafted him. That is not a quarterback problem you solve with a rookie.

The NFL in 1994 was not built for a player like Shuler. Or Shuler was not built for the NFL in 1994. Depends on which side of the failure you're counting.

## What He Did With It

Shuler went home to western North Carolina, got into real estate, ran for Congress, and won. He served three terms representing the 11th District. He was a Blue Dog Democrat who voted against Nancy Pelosi for Speaker twice.

Tennessee gave him the tools to look like something he wasn't quite. The NFL proved it. Washington made it expensive. And Shuler, to his credit, found a different field and stayed on it.

Not every great college player owes you a great pro career.

Some of them just owe you an explanation.
