## The Spread Says One Thing. The Depth Chart Says Another.

Tennessee opens the 2026 season at home against Furman, installed as a 46.5-point favorite at Neyland Stadium. Forty-six and a half points. That number doesn't lie — this program has real talent, real infrastructure, and a fanbase that will show up regardless.

But here's what that number can't tell you: who's running this offense come September 5th.

SEC Network's Roman Harper put it plainly this week. Key positions remain undecided. The Vols have question marks at critical spots on offense, and with Week 1 bearing down, that uncertainty isn't a small thing. It's the whole conversation.

## What We Don't Know Is the Story

Furman is a FCS program. The Paladins are walking into Neyland as 46.5-point underdogs with a 66.5-point over/under. Tennessee is expected to score — a lot. The books are practically begging you to bet the over.

But opener blowouts can be deceiving. Sloppy execution against a cupcake gets laundered into a final score nobody questions. You beat Furman by 40 and suddenly the offense looks settled. It isn't. Not yet.

The uncomfortable question nobody in Vol Nation wants to sit with right now: what happens when the schedule gets real and the offense still doesn't have an identity?

## Harper Named It. Now Someone Has to Fix It.

Roman Harper isn't an alarmist. He's a former SEC safety who played the game at the highest level and calls it straight. When he says Tennessee's offense has unfilled positions and unresolved questions, that's not hot-take radio fodder. That's a credentialed football man reading a depth chart and raising his hand.

The Vols have the athletes. They have the facility. They have the crowd. What they need is clarity — at quarterback or at the skill positions, wherever the fog is thickest — before they walk into a conference schedule that will not be forgiving.

Georgia opens the same week against Tennessee State, rolling out a 45.5-point spread, looking polished and hungry as ever. The SEC's best programs don't wait for their offenses to figure it out. They arrive figured out.

## The Furman Game Is a Dress Rehearsal

Watch the opener not for the score. Watch for rhythm. Watch for who lines up where and whether the playcalling has a pulse. Watch for whether this offense moves like a team that knows what it's doing — or like one still searching.

Furman can't tell you if Tennessee is a contender. But Tennessee's offense can tell you plenty.

The real question isn't whether the Vols win on September 5th. The question is whether they know *who they are* when they do.
