## The Strangest Quarterback Situation in the AFC

No NFL team's quarterback room has a stranger structure right now than Tennessee's.

Cam Ward is the starter. That is not a question. He went 1st overall in the 2025 draft, he has Daboll running the offense for him, and Robert Saleh has staked his first year in Nashville on Ward becoming the player the front office believes he can be.

Mitch Trubisky is the backup. Experienced, known quantity, a coach-friendly presence in the room.

Will Levis is third.

Through the first two preseason games, Will Levis has been the best quarterback on the Tennessee roster. He has completed 84.6% of his passes. He has 153 yards. He has zero turnovers. He is the only one of the three who has been consistently clean.

The roster depth chart is unchanged. Levis is still third.

## What the Numbers Mean and What They Don't

Preseason efficiency for backup quarterbacks is one of the most misleading statistics in football. Backups frequently run against third-string defenders who are themselves fighting for roster spots, with no game plan pressure and no defensive structure to beat. "Looking good in August" against those defenses is not the same as being a starting-caliber NFL quarterback.

Levis knows this. He is not playing against second-team defenses and pretending the results carry over. He is playing because his only option is to keep playing.

The numbers don't change his roster position. They do matter for one specific reason: NFL teams are watching. A quarterback who goes 11-of-13, 153 yards, zero turnovers in a professional preseason game is a quarterback who has options. If Levis is cut in final roster cuts, he will not be unemployed for long.

## Why Tennessee Selected Him Third

Levis was the 33rd overall pick in the 2023 draft. At the time, there was genuine belief that he could develop into a starter. He had the arm. He had the athleticism. The question was always the decision-making — he threw 10 interceptions in his final season at Kentucky, some of which were catastrophic errors in pressure situations.

Tennessee traded for him last spring, believing he could be a legitimate backup behind Ward and that the quality-depth value was worth the cost. He has been exactly that — a professional backup who keeps the room honest and gives Ward someone to compete with in practice.

The organization has not changed its view of Levis as a QB2 at best. His preseason performance has not changed their view. What it has done is confirm that he belongs in the NFL — which he already knew — and that he is worth keeping on a two-quarterback-plus-Trubisky depth chart.

## The Real Fight

Levis's fight is not to displace Cam Ward. That outcome is not available to him.

His fight is to stay on an NFL roster. To not be the answer to a future trivia question about the third quarterback who got cut and disappeared. He was a first-round pick three years ago. He is playing for his professional survival in a preseason game in August of 2026.

That is a brutal thing to witness if you were paying attention to his Kentucky tape or his draft night. It is also a completely ordinary NFL story — the league is full of first-round picks who never made it to Year 4 as starters.

Levis has played smart football in both games. He hasn't taken a sack he didn't have to take. He hasn't forced the ball into coverage. He has been the clean pocket version of himself that the Titans need when Ward is unavailable.

## What Happens in September

Final roster cuts come before Week 1. At that point, Tennessee will decide whether to keep three quarterbacks — Ward, Trubisky, Levis — or cut Levis and go with two.

The salary cap math generally pushes teams toward two active quarterbacks with a third on the practice squad. If Levis clears waivers and accepts a practice squad assignment, Tennessee keeps him close without the cap cost. If another team claims him, he's gone.

His preseason performance makes a practice squad assignment to another team genuinely possible. Whoever he goes to, they're getting someone who is ready to play professional football. He has proven that.

What he hasn't proven, and what this preseason doesn't prove, is that he can run an offense in a game that counts against a defense that's actually trying to stop him. Those reps have been rare in his career. They may have already run out in Tennessee.

The numbers through two games are good. The situation, for Will Levis, is not.
