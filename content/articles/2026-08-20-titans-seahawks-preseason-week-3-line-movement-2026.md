## The Line Doesn't Lie

Tennessee opens as a 4.5-point favorite over Seattle at Nissan Stadium Sunday night. That's a preseason game. Those numbers don't usually look like that.

What's more interesting is where the line *started*. DraftKings opened this thing at Tennessee -1.5. It closed at -4.5. Three full points of movement in a preseason Week 3 game.

Somebody knows something. Or a lot of somebodies do.

## What the Money Is Saying

The moneyline tells the same story. Tennessee opened at -125. Closed at -225. Seattle went from +105 to +185.

That's not casual betting action. That's sharp money with a purpose.

In preseason Week 3, the starters typically play into the second quarter. Sometimes the third. This is the dress rehearsal game — the one coaches actually care about. The one where the bubble guys are fighting for their jobs and the starters are trying to build rhythm before Week 1.

When a line moves three points on a preseason game, you pay attention.

## What It Means for Tennessee

The Titans are 1-0 in the preseason. Road win already banked. Now they come home to Nissan Stadium on a Sunday night with national coverage on FOX.

Seattle is 0-1. They're the visitors. The books think Tennessee is the better team on this field, by a healthy margin.

That either means Tennessee's starters looked sharp in Week 1 and the public noticed. Or Seattle's looked rough. Possibly both.

The total sits at 38.5. Low. That's a nod to the reality of preseason football — vanilla schemes, conservative play-calling, backup quarterbacks making decisions nobody wants them making.

## The Temperature Question

Eighty-two degrees. Mostly sunny. Nashville in August is not a neutral condition.

Seattle plays in a climate where 82 degrees is a heat emergency. Tennessee builds its roster to handle Southern summer heat. That's not nothing in a game where conditioning and depth are already being evaluated.

The weather favors the home side.

## What to Watch

Forget the spread. Watch who's on the field and for how long. Coaches make roster decisions based on what they see Sunday night. Guys fighting for the 53-man roster don't care about the point spread. They care about their jobs.

The line movement suggests the books think Tennessee's personnel — top to bottom — is in better shape heading into the regular season.

That's the real story. Not the score. The depth.

The question worth asking: is Tennessee's roster actually built for something this year, or is this just preseason noise?

We'll know more by kickoff.
