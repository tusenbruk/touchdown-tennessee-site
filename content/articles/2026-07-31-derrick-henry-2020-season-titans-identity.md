## The Number Everyone Misremembers

Derrick Henry rushed for 2,027 yards in 2020. People remember the number. What they forget is the *shape* of it.

Henry didn't grind out 4.2 yards a carry like a committee back doing his job. He averaged 5.4 yards per attempt on 378 carries. He got *better* as defenses schemed specifically to stop him. In the final eight weeks of the regular season, he rushed for over 100 yards six times. The league knew it was coming. He came anyway.

That's not a stat. That's a personality.

## What Mike Vrabel Was Actually Saying

The 2020 Titans ran Henry into defenses that had film, had personnel, and had a plan. They did it 378 times. That's the sixth-most carries in a single season in NFL history.

Vrabel didn't stumble into that number. He chose it.

The Titans that year threw the ball 34th out of 32 teams by design — Ryan Tannehill posted a 106.5 passer rating partly because play-action off Henry's threat turned mid-range throws into layups. The offense was a funnel. Everything ran through 22. Tannehill benefited. A.J. Brown's 1,075 yards benefited. The entire structure of the team benefited from one man's willingness to absorb contact on a schedule.

That's a coaching decision that requires conviction, not creativity.

## The 250-Yard Game Nobody Has Processed Correctly

Week 16, Jacksonville. Henry carried 28 times for 250 yards. The Jaguars were bad. I don't care. You still have to block, you still have to execute, and you still have to break three tackles on a 73-yard run in the fourth quarter when the game is decided and your body has taken 300 carries already.

Henry did it because stopping wasn't an option he recognized.

That game ended with him at 1,777 yards on the season. He needed 250 in the finale against Houston to pass Eric Dickerson's AFC rushing record. He got 250 and two touchdowns. The math required a perfect finish. He delivered a perfect finish.

## What It Said About the Titans

Tennessee's identity in 2020 wasn't sexy. It wasn't spread concepts or analytics-friendly. It was: *we have a weapon no one can stop, and we will make you try until you break.*

That's a franchise statement. That's a front office and a coaching staff saying we believe in physical dominance as strategy, not as fallback.

The Titans lost to the Ravens in the Divisional Round. Henry ran for 40 yards on 18 carries. Baltimore had the answer in January.

But the season still said something true about Tennessee football — that they would rather lose running it their way than win running someone else's.

Some franchises take a decade to figure out what they are. The 2020 Titans knew. They just ran out of season.
