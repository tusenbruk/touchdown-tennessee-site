## The Wrong Job for the Right Man

Mike Munchak earned his gold jacket. Nobody disputes that. Nine Pro Bowls. The anchor of an offensive line that made Earl Campbell and then Eddie George household names. The man understood football at a cellular level.

That's exactly what made his head coaching tenure so instructive — and so quietly devastating.

Tennessee hired him in 2011 after Jeff Fisher's 17-year run finally wore out its welcome. The thinking made sense on the surface. Munchak had been a Titans fixture since the Houston Oilers days. Players respected him. He knew the locker room. He knew the roster.

What nobody asked loudly enough was: does he want this?

## The Coordinator Who Got Promoted to His Own Ceiling

Here's the uncomfortable thing about Munchak. He was a genuinely elite offensive line coach before and after his time as head coach. Pittsburgh hired him after Tennessee let him go, and those Steelers lines were among the better units in the AFC. He won three consecutive assistant coach of the year awards.

So the talent was always there. The work ethic was never the question.

The question was temperament. Great position coaches often have a tunnel vision that makes them brilliant and narrow at the same time. They master one trench, one room, one set of problems. Running a franchise means you're suddenly managing quarterbacks you didn't develop, coordinators with egos, a front office with its own agenda, and a fan base that wants answers on Tuesday after a Sunday loss.

Munchak's press conferences always looked like a man answering questions about someone else's job.

## Three Years, No Playoffs, No Regrets

His record was 22-26. Never finished above .500. The 2011 team went 9-7 and missed the playoffs on a tiebreaker — the high-water mark. After that it eroded. The quarterback situation was never solved. The roster aged. The draft didn't replenish fast enough.

You could blame the front office. You could blame the roster. Some of that criticism lands.

But head coaches find a way or they find the door. Munchak found the door, and then found the job he was actually built for.

## What Tennessee Got Right and Wrong Simultaneously

The Titans hired a legend and got a decent caretaker. That's not an insult — caretakers serve a purpose. The franchise didn't crater. It just didn't grow.

Some men are built to coach one room. Munchak was built to coach *the* room — the offensive line, where everything is physical, technical, and contained.

The real question the Titans should have asked in 2011 wasn't whether Munchak deserved the job. It was whether the job deserved him.

Maybe it didn't.
