## The Loss

Chaz Coleman has been medically disqualified from the 2026 season. The news came out of camp without elaboration — "medical reasons" — and Coleman will not suit up for Tennessee this year.

He was the Vols' best returning edge defender. In 2025, he posted 7.5 sacks and 14 quarterback pressures. He disrupted the run game from the strong side. He was the player Jim Knowles built the spring install around.

When a program loses that kind of player before August ends, the conversation usually goes in one direction. The season is undermined. The defense takes a step back. The question becomes whether Tennessee can survive it.

That framing is understandable. It is also incomplete.

## What Knowles Has Done Before

Jim Knowles was at Penn State in 2023 when they lost three starters before the season began. He was at Ohio State in 2022 when injury depleted the linebacker corps mid-season. His 4-2-5 system is explicitly designed to distribute pass-rush responsibility across multiple players rather than concentrate it in one or two stars.

The philosophy is simple: if you build a defense that requires one person to be dominant for it to function, you have built something fragile. Knowles builds defenses that are structurally sound without superstars — the scheme creates pressure rather than the individual.

In practice, that means Knowles will rotate three or four edge rushers across the same role Coleman occupied. No one player has to replace Coleman's 7.5 sacks. The pressure comes from the system.

## The Players Who Step Into That Pressure

**Jordan Carter** is the immediate name. Carter was described by multiple reporters coming out of spring as the Vols' most consistent edge rusher behind Coleman. He has the frame and the first-step quickness that fits what Knowles asks from the 4-2-5's edge alignment.

Tennessee also brought in reinforcements specifically at the position. The transfer portal additions in the spring addressed exactly this depth question — though the staff clearly didn't know Coleman would be medically disqualified. That decision now looks more prescient than planned.

The depth chart at edge, with Coleman out, runs four players deep. Knowles has said publicly he intends to rotate that group heavily regardless — 50-55 snaps per game distributed across four rather than concentrated in one. Coleman's absence changes the ceiling of the rotation. It doesn't gut it.

## The Honest Concern

Here is the part no one wants to say plainly: Coleman was different.

He had a get-off that none of the players behind him have matched in August. His ability to beat the first blocker on a straight path is something you develop over time, and Carter and the others are still developing it. There will be games — probably against Texas's offensive line, probably against Alabama — where the absence of Coleman's individual burst is visible on the film.

Knowles can scheme to minimize it. He cannot scheme it entirely away.

## What the Schedule Allows

One thing working in Tennessee's favor is timing. The first three games — Furman, at Georgia Tech, Kennesaw State — are the buffer. Carter and the rotation get live reps against competition that is not Texas or Alabama. By the time September 26 arrives at Neyland, Knowles will have three weeks of data on who is competing and who is not.

A player who shows improvement between September 5 and September 26 can become a meaningful pass rusher by October. That is the accelerated development window Knowles is working with.

## What It Means for the Season

Tennessee's 2026 defensive ceiling was always going to be determined by whether Knowles could install his system faster than the opponents could figure it out. Coleman was a large part of what made the ceiling high. Without him, the realistic projection for the pass rush drops — from potentially elite to above-average, with upside.

Above-average pass rush in the SEC is still enough to compete. Jim Knowles built above-average pass rushes at multiple programs without an elite individual edge rusher. He is good at this.

The Vols are not finished without Chaz Coleman. They are different without him. The September questions got harder. The margin for error got smaller. And the next man up now has to answer a question that was supposed to have a different answer.

That is football. You play whoever is available.

Jordan Carter is available.
