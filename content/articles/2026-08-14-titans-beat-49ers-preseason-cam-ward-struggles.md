## The Win Is Fine. Ward Is the Story.

Tennessee walked out of Levi's Stadium with a 19-13 preseason victory Thursday night. Scoreboard says win. Tape says hold on.

Cam Ward — the man this franchise is building around — struggled through three drives against San Francisco's backup defense. Let that sink in. Not the starters. The backups. In August.

Now, I'm not going to burn the building down over a preseason box score. I've covered too many Augusts to do that. But I will ask the uncomfortable question: if Ward can't move the ball against second- and third-stringers in a dress rehearsal, what does that tell you about his command of this offense heading into the regular season?

## The Guys Who Actually Showed Up

While Ward was grinding gears, Julius Chestnut was the most productive Titans player on the field. Five carries, 47 yards. That's nearly ten yards per pop. The man ran with purpose and decisiveness — the kind of running back who makes offensive coordinators sleep well.

Chestnut is fighting for a roster spot. After tonight, he made that fight a whole lot more interesting.

The Niners' passing leader was Adrian Martinez — 16-of-30 for 159 yards. De'Zhaun Stribling hauled in 7 catches for 63 yards on the San Francisco side. Neither of those names is scaring anybody. The fact that Tennessee still gave up 13 points to that group is its own conversation.

## What the Scoreboard Doesn't Tell You

The highlight of the night — literally — was Patrick Taylor Jr. finding a crease up the middle for a touchdown. Old-school, between-the-tackles football. The kind of play that doesn't make SportsCenter but wins games in December.

That's actually the story hiding inside this preseason win. When Ward sputtered, the Titans leaned on the run game and made enough plays to come out ahead. That's not a bad identity to have. But it's a ceiling concern if Ward doesn't develop into the weapon they need him to be.

## The Real Preseason Question

Every Titans fan wants to believe Ward is the answer. He was the pick. The investment. The guy they're handing the franchise to.

But belief has to be fed by evidence. And three rough drives against a secondary that won't make anybody's 53-man roster isn't evidence. It's a warning light.

The Titans won. Good. Now somebody needs to figure out why their franchise quarterback couldn't beat the other team's backups — and fix it before the games count.
