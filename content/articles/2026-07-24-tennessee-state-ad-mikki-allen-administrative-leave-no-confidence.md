## The Situation at TSU

Tennessee State placed athletic director Mikki Allen on administrative leave this week following an internal investigation. The trigger: a formal vote of no confidence from six coaches inside the program.

Six coaches. That's not a grumble in the hallway. That's an organized, documented revolt.

Administrative leave means Allen is out of the building while the investigation runs its course. It does not mean fired — yet. But in college athletics, that distinction rarely holds for long.

## The Timing Makes It Worse

TSU opens the 2026 season on September 5th at Sanford Stadium against Georgia. Kickoff at 3:00 PM on SECN+.

That's a brutal draw under any circumstances — Tennessee State is an FCS program from the Ohio Valley Conference walking into one of the loudest venues in the SEC. Georgia is going to be ready, and the margin for error is basically zero.

Now layer a leadership vacuum on top of that. The athletic director is gone. Six coaches are on record saying they don't trust the administration. That is not a program building toward the Sanford Stadium trip with confidence.

## What a Vote of No Confidence Actually Means

This kind of vote rarely happens in isolation. Coaches don't go on record against their own AD unless they've already decided the relationship is unworkable. It means budget complaints went unanswered. Travel arrangements fell apart. Facilities commitments never materialized. Or something worse.

We don't have the specific allegations yet. The investigation is ongoing. But you don't get six signatures without a sustained pattern of dysfunction.

## The Tennessee Connection

Furman comes to Neyland on the same day TSU plays Georgia. Tennessee's Week 1 setup is straightforward. TSU's is a mess.

Both teams carry "Tennessee" in their name into the first weekend of the season. One program is navigating a warmup game in front of 102,000 orange-clad fans. The other is navigating an institutional crisis while preparing to get off a bus in Athens.

That's a rough way to open a season.

## What Comes Next

The investigation will either produce findings that push for Allen's termination or quietly conclude and put her back in the chair — which would create its own set of problems given how publicly six coaches went on record.

Either way, TSU needs resolution before the season gets any deeper. You can't run an athletic department on administrative leave and a vote of no confidence while your football program is competing.

The real question isn't what happened. It's whether TSU clears this up before it costs them on the field.

Six coaches don't sign that paper unless they've already stopped believing things will get better on their own.
