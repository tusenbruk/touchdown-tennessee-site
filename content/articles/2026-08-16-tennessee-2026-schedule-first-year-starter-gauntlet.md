## Let's Not Pretend This Is an Easy Path

Someone is going to open September 26 under center at Neyland against Texas. That person will have been a college starter for exactly three weeks — Furman, Georgia Tech, and Kennesaw State. The previous entry on their starting resume was zero.

And then they play Texas. Then Auburn. Then Alabama. Then South Carolina. Then Kentucky. Then Texas A&M. Then LSU.

Nine SEC games under the new format. Five of them against programs that finished in the College Football Playoff Top 25 a year ago. That is the curriculum Josh Heupel is handing to a quarterback who, as of today, still hasn't been named the starter.

If they navigate it, Tennessee is a legitimate CFP contender. If they don't, the schedule doesn't get easier — it just keeps coming.

## The Three Games That Define the Season

**Texas at Neyland — September 26**

The SEC opener and the first real measuring-stick moment. Tennessee hosts it, which matters — they're 12-3 at home under Heupel. But Texas is Texas now: full power, full resources, full SEC swagger. This is the game where we find out if the new offense can execute under pressure against a defense that will be well-prepared.

**Alabama at Neyland — October 17**

The Third Saturday in October. Tennessee has beaten Alabama once since the rivalry resumed at its current intensity. The Tide will be the Tide — disciplined, physical, well-coached. The Vols hosting this game is the single biggest structural advantage of the schedule. They need it.

**LSU at Neyland — November 21**

Senior Day. Late-season conference game with potential CFP implications. LSU under Brian Kelly has been inconsistent but always capable of winning games they shouldn't. Playing them at home in front of a crowd that's been building momentum all year is Tennessee's best-case scenario for this matchup.

## The Roadmap to Atlanta

Win Texas and Alabama at home. Survive Arkansas and South Carolina on the road. Get to November healthy.

That is the CFP path. It is not a simple path, but it is a clear one. The schedule is constructed so that the three biggest brand-name opponents — Texas, Alabama, LSU — are all in Knoxville. The road conference games — Arkansas, South Carolina, Texas A&M, Vanderbilt — are winnable, though South Carolina and A&M will require execution.

If Tennessee goes 4-0 in their home SEC games and wins three of four road conference games, they're in the CFP conversation. That's not a prediction — that's math.

## The Early Non-Conference Buffer Is Real

Heupel got lucky with the scheduling geography here. Tennessee has three straight non-conference home games to open the year: Furman (September 5), Georgia Tech (September 12, though that's actually in Atlanta — wait, it's at Georgia Tech), then Kennesaw State at home (September 19), then the SEC season opens immediately.

Actually: Furman at home on September 5, then at Georgia Tech on September 12, then Kennesaw State at home on September 19. Two of the first three are at Neyland. One road trip to Atlanta in Week 2. Before the SEC schedule opens, the quarterback will have experience against multiple defensive schemes and at least one genuine road environment.

It is not three cupcakes. Georgia Tech is not a cupcake. But it is not Texas, Alabama, or LSU either.

## What Eight Ranked Opponents Does to a Quarterback

The 2026 SEC schedule includes five programs that finished in the CFP Top 25 a year ago. The new nine-game format means Tennessee plays one more conference game than it used to — which adds one more ranked opponent to the sample.

A first-year starter going through that slate will face more pressure, more scheme variety, and more hostile environments in one season than most quarterbacks experience across an entire career. If they hold up, they're legitimate. If they struggle, the losses will pile up quickly because the schedule doesn't have many breathers.

## The Honest Assessment

Tennessee has the pieces to compete in every game on this schedule. The offensive line is proven. DeSean Bishop is a genuine SEC weapon at running back. Jim Knowles' defense, if the install holds, should be one of the better units in the conference.

But "compete in" and "win" are different things. The quarterback is the variable that moves Tennessee from "capable of winning the SEC" to "actually winning it." That's not a knock on MacIntyre or Brandon — it's a structural reality.

The schedule doesn't care how ready they feel. It starts September 5 and runs through November 28 at Vanderbilt. Someone is taking every snap in between.

They better be ready.
