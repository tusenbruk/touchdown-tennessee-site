## The Pattern Nobody Names

Safety comes first in Nashville. Always has.

Not safety as in the position. Safety as in the philosophy. The Titans have spent three decades picking players who won't embarrass anybody. High-floor prospects. Scheme fits. Character guys. And when they get it right, they get Steve McNair. When they get it wrong, they get seven years of mediocrity dressed up as stability.

That's the thing nobody says out loud: the hits and misses tell the same story.

## What the Hits Actually Reveal

McNair was 1995. A gamble on a kid from Alcorn State who nobody thought would survive the NFL. That pick looked reckless at the time. It wasn't organizational vision. It was Jeff Fisher trusting a guy who moved like nobody else on film.

Kevin Byard in 2016 was a fifth-round find. Derrick Henry fell to the second round in 2016 because the league undervalued him. Both picks look like genius now. Neither one required much courage in the moment.

That's the uncomfortable truth. The Titans' best picks weren't bold strategy. They were value that other teams left on the table.

## What the Misses Actually Cost

Jake Locker in 2011. Eight overall. The front office drafted the idea of a quarterback — the athleticism, the arm, the intangibles — and skipped past the mechanics that were right there on tape.

Hassan Riddick went 41st in 2012. Zac Brown went 54th in 2012. The Titans took Kendall Wright at 20th. Wright had 94 catches his second year and was out of the league before he turned 30.

This is the real cost. Not just the bad picks. The franchise players drafted by other teams in the same slots.

## The Front Office Doesn't Trust Upside

Here's what the draft history actually reveals: this organization has a ceiling on how much it trusts projection.

They want players who are already close. Finished products. They rarely draft raw athleticism and develop it. They rarely bet on a second or third round pick becoming a star. They play not to lose the draft instead of trying to win it.

Some franchises build on belief. The Titans build on confirmation.

## The Question That Follows You

When JC Latham went 7th overall in 2024, it felt like a safe pick again. Big. Ready. A known commodity.

Maybe that's fine. Maybe that's how this franchise is built.

But somewhere in rounds two through four this April, there's a player who could become something no scout has fully quantified yet.

The question is whether anyone in that war room has the stomach to say so.
