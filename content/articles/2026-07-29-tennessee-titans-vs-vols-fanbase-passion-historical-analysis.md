## The Question Nobody Wants to Answer

Tennesseans hate being asked to choose. Ask a man in Knoxville whether he bleeds Vol orange or Titans navy and he'll dodge it like a third-and-long blitz. But the question matters. Because when you strip away the politeness, one of these fanbases runs to the bone and the other runs to the parking lot.

I've covered this state for thirty years. I know what real looks like.

## What the Vols Built

Tennessee football didn't earn its following. It *inherited* it. Generation after generation, fathers dragged sons to Neyland Stadium the way other families dragged kids to church — not as entertainment, but as obligation. As identity. The 1998 national championship didn't create Vol fans. It just gave them a year they could point to when the rest of the country stopped listening.

The Vols fanbase survived Lane Kiffin leaving for USC after one season. It survived the Derek Dooley years — and Lord, those were years. It survived the Butch Jones era, which felt less like a football program and more like a slow carbon monoxide leak. Fans stayed. They complained loudly, they fired coordinators on Twitter, they ran coaches out of town — but they *stayed*.

That is not a casual relationship. That is a marriage with no exit clause.

## What the Titans Have

The Titans arrived in 1997 and Tennessee welcomed them the way you welcome a nice neighbor — warmly, genuinely, but with limits. The Music City Miracle gave this fanbase a defining moment before it had even fully formed. Steve McNair made them believe. Eddie George made them proud.

But here's what I've noticed over twenty-five years of watching both: Titans fans follow the *team*. Vol fans follow the *program*. There's a difference. One is about wins. The other is about something closer to bloodline.

Titans attendance wobbles with the record. Vol season tickets pass through wills and estates.

## The Honest Truth

This isn't a knock on Titans fans. They're real. They're loud. They filled the old Adelphia stadium in a city that had never known pro football and they made it feel like home.

But if the Titans left tomorrow — packed up and moved to St. Louis or San Antonio — Tennessee would grieve and move on. The way you grieve a good tenant.

If Neyland Stadium went dark? Tennessee wouldn't move on. Tennessee would *collapse*.

That's the difference. One fanbase has roots. The other has cable.

The next question is whether Josh McDaniels and a winning season can change that math. History says probably not.
