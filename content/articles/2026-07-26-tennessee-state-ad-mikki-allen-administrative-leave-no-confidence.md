## The Worst Possible Timing

Tennessee State hasn't played a down of 2026 football yet. Their season opener is September 5th at Sanford Stadium — Georgia, on the road, in front of 93,000 people. That's already a brutal assignment for an FCS program.

Now add this: athletic director Mikki Allen is on administrative leave following an internal investigation triggered by a no-confidence vote from six of the school's own coaches.

Six. Not one disgruntled coordinator with a grudge. Six coaches went on record.

## What a No-Confidence Vote Actually Means

Let's be direct about what this is. A no-confidence vote isn't a complaint filed in a suggestion box. It's a formal, collective declaration that the person running your department has lost the locker room — or in this case, the coaches' offices. When six members of a coaching staff coordinate that kind of action, the underlying grievances aren't minor. They are structural.

We don't have the specifics of the investigation yet. Budget disputes, personnel decisions, lack of support for programs — any of these can fracture an athletic department fast. But the fact that TSU's administration moved quickly to administrative leave tells you the findings had enough weight to act on.

## The Football Program Walks Into a Minefield

Head coach and players now have to prep for a week-one road game against a program that routinely signs top-10 recruiting classes, while their own athletic department is functionally leaderless at the top. That's not a footnote. That's a real operational problem.

Who approves travel logistics? Who signs off on recruiting expenses? Who's the point of contact for scheduling decisions that affect week two and beyond? Athletic departments don't run on autopilot when the AD chair is empty.

The Tigers' football staff is trying to game-plan Georgia's defensive front, and instead they're watching their institution sort out an administrative fire drill.

## What Comes Next

TSU will name either an interim AD or clarify Allen's status before the season kicks off — they have to. The program can't operate in limbo with games on the schedule.

But the damage here isn't just administrative. Recruiting is relational. Prospects and their families ask about program stability. A public no-confidence vote and an AD on leave is not the story you want circulating in living rooms across the country when you're trying to build.

The Tigers open against Georgia on SECN+. The scoreboard will be ugly regardless.

The question is whether the front office is stable enough to survive what comes after it.
