## The Hire That Changed Everything

When Mike Vrabel left, Tennessee needed a different kind of head coach. They got one.

Robert Saleh was introduced on January 30, 2026, as the 22nd head coach in franchise history. He brings 21 seasons of NFL coaching experience — Houston, Seattle, Jacksonville, San Francisco, New York. He was the architect of the 49ers' 4-3 defensive system under Kyle Shanahan. He ran the Jets for four seasons.

What matters most for Nashville isn't his resume. It's his philosophy. Saleh is installing an aggressive, rotation-heavy defensive scheme and plans to call the defensive plays himself — simultaneously serving as head coach and de facto defensive coordinator. That is not a common arrangement. It tells you what he values and where he believes he can make the most direct impact.

The defensive line is where that impact starts.

## Saleh's Defensive Design

Jeffery Simmons is back. He is the best interior defensive player in the AFC South and one of the better defensive tackles in the conference. Saleh watched him in 2025 and made him the foundation of everything the defense will try to do.

Then he went and got John Franklin-Myers.

Franklin-Myers signed a three-year, $63 million deal on March 12, coming from Washington. He and Simmons represent arguably the best interior pass-rushing tandem in the conference. DC Gus Bradley praised the pairing immediately, calling them players who "attack things" the same way.

Saleh's scheme targets about 60% of snaps for the starters and rotates the line heavily after that. The theory: a fresh defensive lineman in the fourth quarter outperforms a tired one who has played 80% of snaps. If the rotation holds, Tennessee's pass rush should be better in the fourth quarter than in the first — which is exactly backwards from most NFL defensive lines.

To complement the interior, Tennessee added Keldric Faulk and Jeremiah Johnson II via trade, plus Jacob Martin from Washington and Malik Herring from Kansas City. The edge depth is real.

## What Daboll Is Doing on the Other Side

Brian Daboll was fired from the Giants in November 2024 after going 20-40-1 as a head coach. That record obscures his actual coaching ability. As Bills offensive coordinator, he was the 2022 NFL Coach of the Year. More importantly, he is the coach most directly responsible for Josh Allen becoming Josh Allen.

Allen's completion percentage went from 52.8% as a rookie to 63.3% in Year 2 to 69.2% in Year 3. His interception rate dropped steadily. His passer rating climbed. The progression wasn't random — it was the output of a specific developmental program that Daboll ran.

Tennessee hired him to run that program again with Cam Ward.

Daboll's offense is built around spacing, rhythm, and YAC. The quarterback operates in a distributed-decision framework — you see the whole field, you find the open man, you let the scheme create the easy throw. Ward's strengths are improvisation and arm talent. The discipline and processing are what Daboll is adding.

It took three years with Allen. Ward doesn't have three years to show results. But the Year 1 progress marker from the Allen experience was visible enough that Tennessee bet big on Daboll replicating it.

## What Training Camp Has Looked Like

Veterans reported July 28 at Vanderbilt Health Football Center. Saleh went straight into padded, physical practices from Day 1. That is not an accident — he believes the physical standard in August sets the competitive standard in January.

The joint practice sessions are the real evaluation tool. Tennessee practiced with San Francisco on August 11, then played the 49ers two days later. The next joint sessions come against Seattle and Chicago. Three different defenses, three different schemes, three opportunities for Ward and the offense to process something they've never seen before.

Saleh's framing of the preseason: it's for competition, not performance. He's looking for who competes when it's uncomfortable. The early returns on Simmons — described by multiple reporters as "eager" and "nasty" in a Saleh scheme that explicitly rewards aggression — are encouraging.

## The Honest Version of What This Is

Tennessee is not a Super Bowl contender in 2026. The roster isn't there yet. The quarterback is 13 months removed from a 3-14 rookie season. The scheme is being installed for the first time.

What Tennessee is building is a defensively-anchored team that can stay competitive in the AFC South while Cam Ward develops into something better than what he showed last year. Saleh's track record says he can build that kind of defense. Daboll's track record says he can help a young quarterback get better.

If both things happen simultaneously — and there's a reasonable argument that they can — the Titans will win more games than the 4 wins that most preseason projections are assigning them. Maybe not a playoff berth. But a competitive roster that's building toward one.

That's the plan. The preseason is the first test of whether it holds.
