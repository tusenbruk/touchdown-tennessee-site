## The Numbers Were Ugly

August 13, Levi's Stadium, San Francisco. Tennessee Titans 19, San Francisco 49ers 13.

The Titans won. They needed that. What they didn't need was the performance from the most important player on the roster.

Cam Ward: 5 of 12 passing, 57 yards, 56.6 passer rating. Three drives against backup defenders. Multiple outlets called it "scattershot." AtoZ Sports ran with "a real wake-up call." That is not the kind of language anyone in Nashville wanted to see after a first preseason game.

Joey Slye made four field goals, including a 58-yarder and a 55-yarder. Tony Pollard capped the opening drive with a 5-yard touchdown. The defense held its own. The Titans won. Ward was the reason the final score wasn't more comfortable.

## What Actually Happened

Ward's best play of the night was a catch-and-run by Calvin Ridley for about 18 yards. He hit Gunnar Helm on a slip screen for 16 more. Then things tightened. The processing speed wasn't there. The ball placement was inconsistent. He held the ball on plays where the throw needed to come out.

This is not a diagnosis. This is one preseason game against backup defenders in someone else's stadium. Every analysis that follows needs to include that qualifier, because Ward's 2025 tape — which ended in a 3-14 season with a struggling offensive line and no weapons — is the last meaningful data we have on him, and preseason snapshots don't override full-season tape.

What the preseason debut tells you is what was unresolved coming out of training camp. Ward got a live look at pressure from a real front. He processed it the way a player who is still integrating Brian Daboll's system processes it — imperfectly. That's different from not being able to do it.

## What Daboll Is Actually Building

Brian Daboll took Josh Allen in 2018 in Buffalo and spent three years remaking him from a thrower into a decision-maker. Allen's completion percentage went from 52.8% as a rookie to 63.3% to 69.2%. His interception rate dropped. His passer rating jumped. The process was not instant.

Daboll's system is built around spacing and after-catch yardage. It leans on the slot — which is why Wan'Dale Robinson's role matters — and features the tight end as a short-to-intermediate target in rhythmic passing concepts. The quarterback's job is to see the whole field quickly and distribute, not to carry the offense on individual arm talent.

Ward's strengths are arm talent and improvisation. Daboll is asking him to add the distributed-decision-maker layer to those strengths. That process shows up in preseason the way it showed up in Week 1 at Buffalo with Allen: messily.

## Ridley and Tate as the Variable

Calvin Ridley took a pay cut to stay in Nashville. He said during camp: "I like living in Nashville and working with a talented, young group." At 31, with a broken leg having limited him to seven games last year, Ridley is not the statistical centrepiece of this offense. He is the veteran signal-caller for everyone else.

That "everyone else" includes Carnell Tate, the fourth overall pick in the 2026 draft out of Ohio State, who caught 68.8% of contested targets in college. Tate has been everything Tennessee hoped for in camp. But a rookie wide receiver in a new system, working with a second-year quarterback in a new scheme, still has a development timeline. Week 4 against the Steelers will be a better test than anything from the preseason.

## Will Levis, for the Record

Will Levis went 11 of 13 for 153 yards across the first two preseason games. No touchdowns. No interceptions. He is the No. 3 quarterback on this roster behind Ward and Mitch Trubisky.

He is fighting for his NFL career this preseason. His numbers are the best of the three quarterbacks at this stage. That gap between performance and roster position tells you everything about how differently the organization views his upside versus Ward's development trajectory. Being efficient behind a backup offensive line against third-string defenders is not the same as being an NFL starter. He knows that.

## The Bottom Line

Cam Ward had a rough preseason debut. Second-year quarterbacks in new offensive systems having rough preseason debuts is the NFL's equivalent of the sun rising.

The test is not August 13. The test is September and beyond, when Robert Saleh's defense has to hold leads and Daboll's offense has to manufacture points against first-string defenses in games that count.

That's when we find out who Cam Ward is in year two. The preseason gave us a data point. It did not give us the answer.
