## The Number Nobody Talks About

Four of Tennessee's fourteen wins in 1998 were decided by a touchdown or less. Florida. Auburn. Arkansas. Mississippi State. Each one a slow leak. Each one plugged.

People remember Tee Martin. They remember Peerless Price against Florida State in the Fiesta Bowl. They remember Al Wilson standing over somebody and screaming. What they don't spend enough time on is what it means to win four close ones in a row and not flinch once.

That's the actual story.

## What Phillip Fulmer Built

Fulmer caught grief his whole career for being a program manager rather than a genius. Fair enough. He wasn't going to out-scheme anybody. But that 1998 team had something specific he installed: they believed the game was never over.

That sounds like a bumper sticker. It wasn't.

Down seven to Florida with under three minutes left in Knoxville — a Florida team that had beaten Tennessee four straight years — Tee Martin drove ninety-one yards. He wasn't spectacular that day. He was steady. There's a difference, and most quarterbacks that age don't know it.

Martin got overshadowed by Peyton Manning leaving a year early. He spent all of 1998 being the guy who wasn't Peyton. He handled it. That's actually harder than it sounds when you're twenty-one years old in Neyland Stadium.

## The Defense Nobody Romanticizes

Al Wilson gets the flowers. Deserves them. But that defense gave up exactly seventeen points in the final five games of the season. Seventeen. Combined.

John Henderson wasn't eligible yet. Darwin Walker was a sophomore. Deon Grant was back there doing things in the secondary that don't show up in highlights because they're the plays where nothing happens.

The best defenses in Tennessee history tend to look boring on film. This one was boring. Boring is underrated.

## What Made Them Different

Every great team has talent. The 1998 Vols had something rarer: they had players who understood their role and didn't audition for a bigger one.

Peerless Price ran the right route on the Fiesta Bowl touchdown because that was his route. He didn't freelance. He trusted the call. The ball was there.

That's a small thing. That's everything.

Tennessee hasn't been back to that conversation since. The question worth sitting with isn't what that team had. It's what every team since has been missing — and whether it's talent, or something quieter than that.
