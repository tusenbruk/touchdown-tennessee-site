## The Trophy Wasn't the Point

Eddie George picked up the Heisman in December 1995 and everybody assumed they knew the story. Big back. Great season. Next chapter.

They were wrong.

The Heisman was the prologue. Nashville was the novel.

## What People Forget About That Draft

George went fourteenth overall in 1996. The Oilers — not yet the Titans, still dragging their Houston identity north through Memphis and into Tennessee — needed more than a running back. They needed a reason for people to care.

George gave them one.

He rushed for over 1,300 yards as a rookie. Then did it again. Then again. Four straight thousand-yard seasons before the new millennium even arrived. He was the engine of a franchise that hadn't yet earned its zip code.

Here's the thing people don't sit with long enough: Jeff Fisher built that team around George the way Bear Bryant built around the wishbone. Not because George was the most explosive back in the league — he wasn't — but because he was *reliable in a way that franchises are built on*. He didn't disappear in January. He got bigger.

## The Music City Miracle Was His, Too

Everybody remembers Lorenzo Neal. Everybody remembers Frank Wycheck's lateral. Nobody talks enough about the fact that Eddie George had already willed that team through a brutal regular season to get to that moment.

He rushed for 88 yards in that wild card game against Buffalo. Workmanlike. Quiet. Exactly what he always was.

## The Super Bowl No One Forgets

Super Bowl XXXIV. One yard short. Kevin Dyson reaching.

George carried the ball 28 times that night for 95 yards against the greatest show on turf. The Rams were a video game. The Titans were a man's fist through a wall.

That game broke something in Titans fans. But it also *defined* them. And George was at the center of it — not glorious, not victorious, but *present*. Grinding. Refusing.

That's legacy.

## What He Actually Left Behind

George finished with 10,009 career rushing yards. He started 128 consecutive games at running back — a number that sounds impossible in today's NFL.

But forget the numbers for a second.

The Titans were a franchise without roots, without history in Tennessee, without a Super Bowl ring. George gave them identity during the years when identity was the only thing that mattered.

Heisman winners come along every year.

Somebody who becomes the *face* of a rootless franchise trying to become something real — that's a different category of player entirely.

The question isn't whether Eddie George belongs in the Titans Ring of Honor. He does, and he's there.

The question is whether we've actually reckoned with what this city would look like without those eight years of his shoulders.
