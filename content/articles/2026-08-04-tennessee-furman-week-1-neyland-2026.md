## The Opponent on the Ticket

Furman is a FCS program out of Greenville, South Carolina. The Paladins play in the Southern Conference. They are not Georgia. They are not Florida. They are the kind of opponent you schedule when you want your offensive line to work out some rust without risking a headline.

Tennessee opens at home on September 5th, 3:30 PM kickoff, on SECN+. That last part matters. SECN+ is where games go when the network doesn't want them in the living room. It streams. Your uncle can't find it. Half the 102,000 seats at Neyland will be occupied by people who drove in from Blount County anyway, so it doesn't much matter.

## What This Game Actually Is

Call it what it is: an installation week. The coaches get to see who can execute an assignment at full speed. The starters play until halftime, maybe into the third quarter if something isn't clicking. The backup quarterback throws three passes. The true freshmen get some snaps. Someone pulls a hamstring that becomes a storyline for two weeks.

This is not cynicism. This is how September 5th works.

Vivid Seats has tickets starting at $47. There are 3,792 available. That's not a sellout problem — Neyland holds over 100,000. That's just the secondary market moving slow in early August on a game that doesn't exactly have national intrigue.

## The More Interesting Game Happening Simultaneously

Down in Athens, Georgia is hosting Tennessee State — the *other* Tennessee, the one from Nashville — at the same time, also on SECN+. That game is less meaningful to Vol fans but contains its own quiet irony. Tennessee State is an HBCU program from a city that Tennessee fans don't spend a lot of time thinking about. Georgia scheduled them. Fine.

The Vols didn't.

## What To Watch For

Furman ran the ball reasonably well in 2025 against mid-level FCS competition. They are not a pushover the way Murray State — playing MTSU that same evening — is a pushover. But they are not a test.

The real question on September 5th isn't whether Tennessee wins. They will. The question is whether the offensive line looks like a unit or a collection of individuals. Whether the new pieces on defense communicate. Whether the staff got what it wanted out of fall camp.

You won't know any of that until you watch the first three drives.

Furman just happens to be on the other sideline when it happens.
