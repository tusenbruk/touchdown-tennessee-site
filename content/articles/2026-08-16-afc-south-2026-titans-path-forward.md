## The Division No One Can Call

The AFC South has been the most predictably chaotic division in professional football for the better part of a decade. Jacksonville has won it. Tennessee has won it. Houston has won it. Indianapolis has come close. Every year someone from this division exceeds expectations and someone else collapses.

In 2026, four teams have genuine question marks entering the season. That is not a reason to dismiss Tennessee's chances. It is a reason to take them more seriously than the preseason lines suggest.

## Houston Is Not as Safe a Pick as the Moneyline Says

The Texans are the consensus AFC South favourite at +135. The defensive case for that is airtight: Will Anderson Jr. and Danielle Hunter combined for 27 sacks, 45 quarterback hits, and 35 tackles for loss in 2025. Both received new contracts. Derek Stingley Jr. runs the secondary. The defense is genuinely elite.

The offensive case has cracks.

C.J. Stroud turned the ball over seven times across two postseason games last year — four first-half interceptions against New England. That is not a statistical blip. That is a pattern of pressure performance that will define how far Houston goes in 2026. If Stroud holds the football, they're legitimate Super Bowl contenders. If he reverts to the postseason version of himself, they're a one-and-done team with a great defense.

Houston also added a rookie (Keylan Rutledge) competing at center and left guard. The offensive line is in transition at exactly the spots that determine whether the quarterback gets time to deliver the ball clean. That's a risk the moneyline doesn't fully account for.

## Jacksonville Is More Dangerous Than the National Media Is Giving Them Credit For

The Jaguars won the AFC South in 2025 at 13-4. They were better than their divisional record because they were more physical than anyone expected. They return Trevor Lawrence with what everyone inside the building says is the best version of his chemistry with Brian Thomas Jr.

Lawrence's words during camp: "Our chemistry is the best it's been since we've been together."

Thomas was the team's most important offensive player in the second half of last season. If he is what his 2025 tape suggests — a genuine No. 1 receiver who can win contested situations and separate underneath — and Lawrence has another year of development, Jacksonville is a legitimate conference title contender.

Their running game is the question. Travis Etienne is gone. Whoever fills that role will be asked to carry more load than the roster depth currently projects. Offensive coordinator Liam Coen wants to get downfield more, which is a scheme preference that also puts more pressure on Lawrence to deliver under coverage.

## Indianapolis Has a Real Question That Has Nothing to Do With Daniel Jones' Arm

Daniel Jones coming back from an Achilles tear is the biggest individual storyline in the AFC South this summer. He was "cleared to do everything" and described as "on fire" in the Colts' camp scrimmage. The physical recovery appears genuine.

The question isn't whether Jones can throw the football. It's whether the Achilles holds up through a 17-game season. Achilles tears have a documented track record of affecting performance 12-18 months post-surgery even in players who pass training camp with no issues. The Colts are betting their 2026 season on Jones clearing that hurdle.

If he does, Indianapolis has enough around him to compete. If he re-aggravates the injury in Week 6, Riley Leonard is their quarterback, and that is a different team entirely.

## Tennessee's Actual Case

Here is the Tennessee Titans 2026 case, made honestly:

The offensive line is better than it was in 2025. Kevin Zeitler's signing was praised as one of the best moves of the offseason. The line around Cam Ward is more complete than the one he was behind during his 3-14 rookie season.

Carnell Tate is the fourth overall pick, and his camp tape has been everything the organization hoped. Calvin Ridley is healthy and motivated. Wan'Dale Robinson is a reliable slot option. Gunnar Helm at tight end gives Ward a safety valve.

The defense, if Saleh's rotation philosophy holds, should be among the better units in the AFC South. Simmons and Franklin-Myers at the interior. Edge additions across the board. Saleh calling the plays himself.

That is a legitimately different roster from the one that went 3-14 in Ward's rookie year.

## The Realistic Number

Tennessee isn't winning the AFC South this year. The projection for where the regression-to-competitive-average lands is probably 7-8 wins. Enough to make it interesting in December, not enough to put genuine pressure on Houston or Jacksonville for the division title.

But 7 wins in the AFC South with a second-year quarterback and a new coaching staff in its first year? That's ahead of schedule. That's a program building toward something. And it's better than the 4-5 wins that most preseason projections are assigning them.

The AFC South is going to be close. It is going to produce a wild card team from the second place. If Tennessee can win the games they're supposed to win and steal one they shouldn't, they're in that conversation.

That is not a prediction. It is a possibility the odds haven't fully accounted for.
