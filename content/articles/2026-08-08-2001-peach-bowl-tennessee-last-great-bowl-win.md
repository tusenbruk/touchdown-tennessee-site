## Atlanta, December 29, 2001

Casey Clausen took the first snap of his bowl career against Michigan and didn't flinch. That should have meant something. At the time it just looked like Tennessee winning again.

Final score: Tennessee 45, Michigan 17. Atlanta. The Peach Bowl. A game that got filed under *routine* by people who had watched the Vols play in January for most of the previous decade.

That was the problem with the filing system.

## What the Score Obscured

Phillip Fulmer was 53-13 in his last five seasons entering that game. The program had won a national title in 1998. It had won ten games in four straight years. The Peach Bowl felt like maintenance, not a milestone.

But look at the roster sheet. Donte Stallworth was finishing his last game as a Vol. Travis Stephens rushed for 106 yards and would be gone. The offensive line that had carried that era was walking out the door in chunks.

Clausen was 19 years old and playing his fourth college game. Tennessee was in transition and calling it continuity.

## The Thing Nobody Said Afterward

The Vols had beaten a 8-4 Michigan team by four touchdowns. Nobody threw a parade, but nobody worried either. The assumption — unspoken, institutional — was that this was the floor. That Tennessee would keep being Tennessee.

That assumption was wrong in ways that took years to become obvious.

The 2002 season ended without a bowl win. So did 2003. The 2004 Cotton Bowl loss to Texas A&M. The 2005 Capital One Bowl loss to Wisconsin. Fulmer's tenure ended in 2008. Then Kiffin. Then Dooley. Then Jones. Sixteen years passed before Tennessee won another bowl game of consequence.

Sixteen.

## What Clausen Saw From the Field

He completed 15 of 24 passes that day. Two touchdowns. No interceptions. He looked like the answer. And honestly, for stretches, he was.

But answers in football have expiration dates, and nobody in the Georgia Dome that afternoon was reading the label.

## Why It Matters Now

Tennessee fans who remember 2001 remember feeling solid. Settled. Like the program had a permanent address in the top fifteen.

That feeling is what makes the 2001 Peach Bowl worth revisiting — not the performance, but the posture. The ease of it. The assumption buried inside every handshake in the locker room afterward.

Casey Clausen jogged off that field thinking he had nine more bowl games ahead of him.

He got two.

The question isn't what went wrong after Atlanta. The question is whether anyone was paying close enough attention to see it coming.
